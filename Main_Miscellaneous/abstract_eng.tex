\begin{englishabstract}
	
	Fast-reactor mixed oxide (MOX) fuel operates under conditions of high power density, steep temperature gradients, and intense neutron irradiation, under which pore migration and microstructural restructuring, actinide redistribution, evolution of oxygen stoichiometry, and fission gas release occur within the fuel. These processes interact strongly with fuel--cladding gap heat transfer and nonlinear mechanical response, giving rise to pronounced multiphysics coupling. Existing fuel performance analysis methods remain limited in providing a unified description of these microstructural evolution processes and their cross-scale feedback, while the high computational cost of multidimensional high-fidelity finite-element models constrains their application to large-scale operating-condition analysis and online state assessment. Focusing on fast-reactor MOX fuel elements, this work employs the MOOSE multiphysics finite-element framework to investigate material properties and in-reactor behavior modeling, microstructural restructuring and constituent redistribution, thermo--fission-gas--mechanical coupling algorithms, fuel-rod-level validation, and rapid prediction of two-dimensional thermo-mechanical fields.
	
	First, the material-property correlations and in-reactor behavior models required for oxide fuel-element analysis are implemented. For MOX fuel, these include correlations for thermal conductivity, specific heat capacity, thermal expansion, and other thermophysical properties, together with elastic constitutive relations and models for creep, densification, swelling, and relocation. For the cladding, thermophysical-property correlations and in-reactor mechanical behavior models are established for representative fast-reactor cladding materials, including SS316, D9, and HT9. The fuel--cladding gap heat-transfer model accounts for multicomponent gas conduction, solid--solid contact conduction, and thermal radiation. To describe fuel microstructural evolution under steep temperature gradients, a pore transport equation is solved, with pore migration velocities evaluated using both empirical correlations and the Rand--Markin multicomponent thermodynamic model. Stabilized discretization schemes are introduced for convection-dominated problems to improve numerical stability. For constituent redistribution, the Pu transport model simultaneously accounts for chemical diffusion, thermal diffusion, and pore-migration effects, and the same framework is extended to Am transport. The evolution of the oxygen-to-metal ratio (O/M) is modeled by coupling thermal and chemical diffusion. The simulations reproduce pore migration toward high-temperature regions, enrichment of Pu and Am near the fuel center, and radial oxygen redistribution under different initial O/M conditions, while capturing the bidirectional coupling among microstructure, constituent distribution, thermochemical state, and temperature field.
	
	To address the strong nonlinear coupling encountered in fuel performance calculations, the finite-element discretization of the thermo-elasto-plastic governing equations is formulated, and a radial return algorithm is employed to implicitly update stresses and inelastic state variables at material integration points. Fission gas behavior is described using both the ForMas model based on the Forsberg--Massih theory and the grain-scale mechanistic code SCIANTIX. Through dedicated coupling interfaces, fission gas release, rod internal pressure, gas composition, gap heat transfer, and fuel deformation are incorporated into a closed-loop coupled computational framework. The numerical implementation and coupling are verified through single-element tests of material models and dedicated fission-gas and gap heat-transfer cases. Fuel-rod-level validation is further performed using publicly available MOX fuel irradiation data. The calculated fuel centerline temperatures generally reproduce the experimental trends with power and burnup. The analysis indicates that fuel thermal-conductivity degradation, gap evolution, fission gas release, and contact heat transfer are major factors affecting temperature prediction at high burnup. These results demonstrate the necessity of solving temperature, fission gas release, and gap heat transfer within a mutually coupled feedback loop.
	
	To reduce the online computational cost of the high-fidelity model, a POD--LSTM-based rapid full-field prediction method is further developed for variable power histories. Temperature, radial displacement, and axial displacement fields are generated using a unified two-dimensional axisymmetric model. Proper orthogonal decomposition (POD) reduces the 17,151-dimensional multi-field nodal output to a 28-dimensional POD coefficient vector, while a long short-term memory (LSTM) network learns the temporal mapping from power histories to the evolution of the POD coefficients. The dataset consists of 40 training histories, 10 validation histories, and 10 independent blind-test histories. For the blind-test set, the mean relative $L_2$ errors of temperature, radial displacement, and axial displacement are 1.256\%, 1.528\%, and 2.549\%, respectively. For a single operating history containing 109 time steps, the average total time required for POD-coefficient prediction and reconstruction of the three physical fields is approximately 0.0743~s. Within the range covered by the training conditions, the proposed method accurately predicts the complete thermo-mechanical fields of fast-reactor MOX fuel and provides an efficient reduced-order tool for large-scale operating-history analysis and digital-twin-oriented online state assessment.
	
	This study establishes an integrated methodological framework that links microstructural and constituent evolution in fast-reactor MOX fuel, high-fidelity multiphysics coupled analysis, and rapid prediction of complete thermo-mechanical fields. The developed framework enhances the capability of fuel performance analysis to represent microstructural evolution and its cross-scale feedback, while providing a technical basis for extending high-fidelity fuel performance models from offline detailed simulations to rapid operating-history analysis and online state assessment. The results provide theoretical and methodological support for analyzing the in-reactor behavior of fast-reactor MOX fuel elements, improving and validating fuel performance models, assessing operating states, and advancing digital-twin-oriented fuel performance prediction.
